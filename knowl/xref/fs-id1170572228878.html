Example 5.3.8 Using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus with Two Variable Limits of Integration.

Let \(F(x)=\displaystyle{ \int_{x}^{2x} t^3\, dt}.\) Find \(F'(x) .\)
🔗
Solution.
We have \(F(x)=\displaystyle{ \int_{x}^{2x} t^3dt}.\) Both limits of integration are variable, so we need to split this into two integrals. We get
🔗
\begin{align*} F(x)\amp =\displaystyle{ \int_{x}^{2x} t^3\, dt}\\ \amp =\displaystyle{ \int_{x}^{0} t^3\, dt}+\displaystyle{ \int_{0}^{2x} t^3\, dt}\\ \amp =- \displaystyle{ \int_{0}^{x} t^3\, dt}+\displaystyle{ \int_{0}^{2x} t^3\, dt}. \end{align*}
Differentiating the first term, we obtain
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{d}{dx}\left[- \displaystyle{ \int_{0}^{x} t^3\, dt}\right]=- x^3. \end{equation*}
Differentiating the second term, we first let \(u(x)=2x.\) Then,
🔗
\begin{align*} \frac{d}{dx}\left[\displaystyle{ \int_{0}^{2x} t^3\, dt}\right]\amp =\frac{d}{dx}\left[\displaystyle{ \int_{0}^{u(x)} t^3\, dt}\right]\\ \amp =(u(x))^3\frac{du}{dx} \\ \amp =(2x)^3\cdot 2\\ \amp =16x^3. \end{align*}
Thus,
🔗
\begin{align*} F'(x) \amp =\frac{d}{dx}\left[- \displaystyle{ \int_{0}^{x} t^3\, dt}\right]+\frac{d}{dx}\left[\displaystyle{ \int_{0}^{2x} t^3dt}\right]\\ \amp =- x^3+16x^3\\ \amp =15x^3. \end{align*}
