Example 5.5.10 Finding an Antiderivative Using \(u\)-Substitution.

Use substitution to find \(\displaystyle \int \frac{x}{\sqrt{x-1}}\, dx.\)
🔗
Solution.
If we let \(u=x-1,\) then \(du=dx.\) But this does not account for the \(x\) in the numerator of the integrand. We need to express \(x\) in terms of \(u\text{.}\) However, given our choice \(u=x-1,\) we can solve for \(x\) to obtain \(x=u+1.\) Now we can rewrite the integral entirely in terms of \(u\text{:}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} \int \frac{x}{\sqrt{x-1}}\, dx\amp = \int \frac{u+1}{\sqrt{u}}\, du\\ \amp = \int \sqrt{u}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{u}}\, du\\ \amp = \int u^{1/2}+u^{-1/2}\, du. \end{align*}
Then we integrate in the usual way, replace \(u\) with the original expression, and factor and simplify the result. Thus,
🔗
\begin{align*} \int u^{1/2}+u^{-1/2}\, du\amp =\dfrac{2}{3}u^{3/2}+2u^{1/2}+C\\ \amp =\dfrac{2}{3}(x-1)^{3/2}+2(x-1)^{1/2}+C\\ \amp =(x-1)^{1/2}\left(\dfrac{2}{3}(x-1)+2\right)+C\\ \amp =(x-1)^{1/2}\left(\frac{2}{3}x-\frac{2}{3}+\frac{6}{3}\right)\\ \amp =(x-1)^{1/2}\left(\dfrac{2}{3}x+\frac{4}{3}\right)\\ \amp =\dfrac{2}{3}(x-1)^{1/2}(x+2)+C. \end{align*}
