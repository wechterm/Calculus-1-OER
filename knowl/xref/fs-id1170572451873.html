Example 5.3.22 Finding the Point Where a Function Takes on Its Average Value.

Given \(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{3} x^2\, dx=9,\) find \(c\) such that \(f(c)\) equals the average value of \(f(x)=x^2\) over \([0,3].\)
🔗
Solution.
We are looking for the value of \(c\) such that
🔗
\begin{equation*} f(c)=\dfrac{1}{3-0} \int_{0}^{3} x^2\, dx=\frac{1}{3}(9)=3. \end{equation*}
Replacing \(f(c)\) with \(c^2\text{,}\) we have
🔗
\begin{align*} c^2\amp = 3\\ c\amp = \pm \sqrt{3}. \end{align*}
Since \(- \sqrt{3}\) is outside the interval, take only the positive value. Thus, \(c=\sqrt{3}\) (Figure 5.3.23).
