Example 4.9.9. When Newton’s Method Fails.

Consider the function \(f(x)=x^3-2x+2\text{.}\) Let \(x_0=0\text{.}\) Show that the sequence \(x_1,x_2,\ldots\) fails to approach a root of \(f\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
For \(f(x)=x^3-2x+2\text{,}\) the derivative is \(f'(x)=3x^2-2\text{.}\) Therefore,
\begin{equation*} x_1=x_0-\frac{f(x_0)}{f'(x_0)}=1-\frac{f(1)}{f'(1)}=1-\frac{1}{1}=0\text{.} \end{equation*}
In the next step,
\begin{equation*} x_2=x_1-\frac{f(x_1)}{f'(x_1)}=1-\frac{f(1)}{f'(1)}=1-\frac{1}{1}=0\text{.} \end{equation*}
Consequently, the numbers \(x_0,x_1,x_2,\ldots\) continue to bounce back and forth between \(0\) and \(1\) and never get closer to the root of \(f\) which is over the interval \([−2,−1]\) (see Figure 4.9.10). Fortunately, if we choose an initial approximation \(x_0\) closer to the actual root, we can avoid this situation.
