Remark 3.5.4. Radians or Degrees?

It is important to note that the derivation of the derivatives of \(\sin\) and \(\cos\) required the limits from Subsection 2.4.6, and while proving whether those limits existed, we needed Figure 2.4.31. The measurements in this figure only apply when \(\theta\) has radian units. Therefore, the derivative formulas for our trigonometric functions are only valid with radian values of \(x\)
🔗
(To put it another way, we know that \(\ds\lim_{\theta\to 0}\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta}=1\) when \(\theta\) is in radians. But see if this still works when the input is in degrees. Use a calculator to plug in small degree values for \(\ds\frac{\sin\theta^\circ}{\theta^\circ}\) and find that the result is not approaching \(1\text{!}\)
