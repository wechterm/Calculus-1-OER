Example 3.9.21. Extending the Power Rule.

Find the derivative of \(y=x^r\) where \(r\) is an arbitrary real number.
🔗
Solution.
The process is the same as in Example 3.9.20, though with fewer complications.
🔗
\begin{align*} \ln y\amp=\ln x^r\amp\amp\text{ Step 1. Take the natural logarithm of both sides. }\\ \ln y\amp=r \ln x\amp\amp\text{ Step 2. Expand using properties of logarithms. }\\ \frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx}\amp= r\frac{1}{x}\amp\amp\text{ Step 3. Differentiate both sides. }\\ \frac{dy}{dx}\amp=y\frac{r}{x}\amp\amp\text{ Step 4. Multiply by } y \text{ on both sides. }\\ \frac{dy}{dx}\amp=x^r\frac{r}{x}\amp\amp\text{ Step 5. Substitute } y=x^r.\\ \frac{dy}{dx}\amp= rx^{r- 1}\amp\amp\text{ Simplify. } \end{align*}
