Example 5.2.17 Finding the Net Signed Area.

Find the net signed area between the curve of the function \(f(x)=2x\) and the \(x\)-axis over the interval \([-3,3].\)
🔗
Solution.
The function produces a straight line that forms two triangles: one from \(x=-3\) to \(x=0\) and the other from \(x=0\) to \(x=3\) (Figure 5.2.18). Using the geometric formula for the area of a triangle, \(A=\dfrac{1}{2}bh,\) the area of triangle \(A_{1}\) , above the axis, is
🔗
\begin{gather*} A_1=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot 3 \cdot 6=9 \end{gather*}
where 3 is the base and \(2(3)=6\) is the height. The area of triangle \(A_{2}\) , below the axis, is
🔗
\begin{gather*} A_2=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot 3 \cdot 6=9, \end{gather*}
where 3 is the base and 6 is the height. Thus, the net area is
🔗
\begin{gather*} \displaystyle{\int_{-3}^{3}2x\, dx} =A_1-A_2=9-9=0. \end{gather*}
Analysis
🔗
If \(A_{1}\) is the area above the \(x\)-axis and \(A_{2}\) is the area below the \(x\)-axis, then the net area is \(A_1-A_2.\) Since the areas of the two triangles are equal, the net area is zero.
