Theorem 3.3.6. The Power Rule.

Let \(n\) be a positive integer. If \(f(x)=x^n,\) then
🔗
\begin{equation*} f'(x)=nx^{n-1}. \end{equation*}
Alternatively, we may express this rule as
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{d}{dx}x^n=nx^{n-1}. \end{equation*}
🔗

Proof.
For \(f(x)=x^n\) where \(n\) is a positive integer, we have
🔗
\begin{equation*} f'(x)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{(x+h)^n-x^n}{h}. \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} (x+h)^n=x^n+nx^{n-1}h+{n \choose 2} x^{n-2}h^2+{n \choose 3} x^{n-3}h^3+\text{ … }+nxh^{n-1}+h^n, \end{equation*}
using the Binomial Theorem we see that
🔗
\begin{equation*} (x+h)^n-x^n=nx^{n-1}h+{n \choose 2} x^{n-2}h^2+{n \choose 3} x^{n-3}h^3+\text{ … }+nxh^{n-1}+h^n. \end{equation*}
Next, divide both sides by \(h\text{:}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{(x+h)^n-x^n}{h}=\frac{nx^{n-1}h+{n \choose 2} x^{n-2}h^2+{n \choose 3} x^{n-3}h^3+\text{ … }+nxh^{n-1}+h^n}{h}. \end{equation*}
Thus,
🔗
\begin{equation*} \frac{(x+h)^n-x^n}{h}=nx^{n-1}+{n \choose 2} x^{n-2}h+{n \choose 3} x^{n-3}h^2+\text{ … }+nxh^{n-2}+h^{n-1}. \end{equation*}
Finally,
🔗
\begin{align*} f'(x)\amp=\lim_{h\to 0}(nx^{n-1}+{n \choose 2} x^{n-2}h+{n \choose 3} x^{n-3}h^2+\text{ … }+nxh^{n-1}+h^n)\\ \amp=nx^{n-1}. \end{align*}
